02 / PREMISE

RETAIL COMMUNICATION,
EDITED LIKE A
MAGAZINE.

The Denim Brief began as an independent editorial initiative within Levi's® retail, turning product updates, performance insights, styling knowledge and store initiatives into recurring visual stories.

Designed for the shop floor and published through the internal communication platform, it explored how editorial thinking could make everyday retail information clearer, more relevant and worth engaging with.

Published on YOOBIC · Levi's® internal retail platform

04 / STORE INTELLIGENCE

WORSTS & BLANKS.

Instead of treating underperforming products as a sales report, the format turned them into an editorial starting point.

Products with low sell-through or limited visibility became styling prompts: what could be paired differently, reframed or brought back into the conversation?

05 / RETAIL ACTIVATION

WINTER SALES.

During seasonal sales, the in-store offer was only part of the available product range.

Winter Sales used BackPocket to extend that offer, turning online availability into editorial content that could support conversations with customers on the shop floor.

06 / EDITORIAL SYSTEM

A MAGAZINE
WITHOUT PAGES.

The Denim Brief was never designed as a single publication.

It worked as an evolving editorial system: recurring formats, different voices and visual languages, connected by the same purpose — making retail information worth reading.

ONE RETAIL QUESTION.
DIFFERENT EDITORIAL FORMS.

07 / STORE INTELLIGENCE

BUDGET FIRST.

Christmas shopping often begins with a simple constraint: budget.

Budget First turned that constraint into a practical editorial tool, organising gift suggestions by spending range to help store teams make faster, more relevant recommendations.

CUSTOMER FIRST.
PRODUCT SECOND.

08 / IMPACT

FROM CONTENT
TO BEHAVIOUR.

The Denim Brief was designed to turn internal communication into something store teams could actually use: recurring content connected to products, commercial priorities and everyday conversations with customers.

Rather than treating communication as a one-way announcement, the project tested how editorial content could support attention, participation and selling behaviour on the shop floor.

09 / MY ROLE

EDITORIAL THINKING,
BUILT FROM THE
SHOP FLOOR.

The Denim Brief started from a position inside the store.

Working directly with products, customers and commercial priorities made it possible to observe where information was getting lost — and to turn those observations into recurring editorial formats.

My role moved across research, editorial strategy, art direction and content design: identifying relevant signals, defining formats, producing the visual system and adapting each output to changing store priorities.
